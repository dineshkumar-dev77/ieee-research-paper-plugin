Cloud computing has emerged as a dominant paradigm for hosting distributed services and compute-intensive workflows. Efficient task allocation across heterogeneous virtual machines requires balancing makespan and energy efficiency under service level agreements. Traditional scheduling heuristics, including Min-Min, Max-Min, and Round-Robin, exhibit structural limitations under high-concurrency bursty arrivals. Various heuristics incorporating genetic algorithms and dynamic programming have been proposed; however, scalability in real-time environments remains an active research challenge. Benchmarking methodologies must demonstrate rigorous statistical significance across independent trials.
