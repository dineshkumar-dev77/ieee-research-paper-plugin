In heterogeneous computing environments, resource management hinges on balancing latency, energy consumption, and quality-of-service guarantees. Traditional static heuristics often fail to adapt to bursty arrival patterns and non-stationary task workloads. By formulating the scheduling problem as an online constrained optimization, dynamic heuristics can achieve superior makespan reduction without incurring prohibitive computational overhead. Furthermore, empirical characterization under realistic system variations confirms that adaptive decision boundaries remain robust against queuing delays.
